Introduction

In fourth grade, I had a great idea. It was the era when environmentalism was becoming mainstream, and hybrid Prius’ were taking over the roads. Every day, I saw the mailmen pass our house in their gas-powered trucks and I thought - hey, why don’t we make our mail trucks hybrid? Postal trucks drive in an extremely fuel-inefficient manner with all those starts and stops. So while switching to a hybrid fleet would be a large upfront investment for the government, it would definitely pay off in the long term. I felt so passionately about this issue that I wrote directly to the Postmaster General (in a 5-paragraph essay) about why we should invest in hybrid postal trucks. Week after week, I waited for a response - anything. I was so excited by the prospect of having a voice on this topic. My parents, cynically but correctly, warned me that I might not receive a response, and after a couple of months, I realized they were right. 

My interest in government & the people they serve thankfully did not die out from that demoralizing interaction. But it has been trying, to say the least, to remain optimistic. A master’s degree, several internships, and several more unanswered letters (& emails) to government officials later, I’ve decided to embark on my own journey to figure out - how do I actually interact with my government? What can I or should I expect? 

In civic-minded circles, I hear the same ask all the time - engage in your local community. Call your legislators, go to your city council meetings, tell them what you care about. That’s the only way, people say, to make change. But I find that ask a bit hypocritical when the engagement is only one way. Would you stay friends with someone who never texted you back? Engagement is a two-way street. And it should be easier to see & connect the impact we have on our government (and that the government has on us) in our everyday lives. 

This work is so important because not everyone has the time, energy, or technical skills to navigate their way through the government services maze. And this is likely most true for those who rely on government services the most. This group should have the loudest voice in shaping how government services impact them, but that is not the case today.

The goal of this project is to prototype different solutions that answer my questions - as an average interested local resident - about how the local government impacts my neighborhood, and how I can impact my local government. The hope is that any research, learnings, or tools from this project can inform and inspire the development of tools that better connect the city & its residents. I do want to acknowledge a lot of the great work the civic tech community has done in this space, trying to solve this exact problem. Any work done here is only possible because of the digital infrastructure this community has built over the past 20 years. 

Any tools or analyses that I build as a part of this journey will be public for anyone to use, and I hope it can help others more clearly & easily see & improve how their government can work for them. 

If you’re also interested in this topic & want to chat - please reach out to me! 
If you just want to follow along, you can subscribe to any new posts I publish here.


The plan
Timeline: Ideally, I want to work in sprints (~2 weeks), knowing the timeline will almost definitely change as I learn more information.

Who’s the persona? 
Local resident who has access to the internet & cell phone and has little to no understanding of how the government works. 
Curious about this topic, but has little to no time to spend researching this themselves.  

What are my goals? 
To create research & tools so that the persona should be able to easily understand: 
Part 1: What in my neighborhood does the city directly impact? 
Part 2:  How does the city impact what goes on in my neighborhood? 
Part 3:  What should I do if I want to change how the city is impacting my neighborhood?
I’ll dive into each of the 3 parts in order above, with subquestions likely to follow as I learn more about the space. 

Huge caveat: Everyone has very different ways that they engage with their city, and this is only addressing my (n=1) viewpoint. If you have a perspective you’d like to share with me to add to this project, please reach out to me. I’d love to learn more about your experience! 

Methodology: 
For each of the questions above: 
Research & synthesize what’s already out there. 
Build additional tools if necessary 
Note: I’ll be using Claude Code to help with the build. These are prototypes so they are not ready to be scaled, should not store sensitive user data, etc. 
Pressure test my understanding with experts - talk to local elected officials, people in the system who understand how it works
Get feedback from the community - what is improved, what else is missing? 
Iterate based on feedback
